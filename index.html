How this works

The whole planner in one page, in plain words.

1 — The one number

Everything serves one number: what you can safely spend today. It is worked out like this — start with the money in the accounts you choose to count. Take away every bill due before payday, the minimum debt payments due before payday, anything you set aside for savings, and your buffer. What is left is divided by the days until payday. That is today's number, and it never goes below zero.

A small example: 1,000 in the bank − 200 of bills coming − 50 of minimum payments − 25 buffer = 725 left to last, ÷ 5 days = 145 a day. Spend 20 at lunch and today's number becomes 125 — honest, not punishing. Tap “How is this worked out?” on the Today screen any time to see your own real figures.

2 — The everyday loop

Check your balance in your banking app, then tap “Check my money” and confirm it here — that keeps the number honest. Log spends with the quick-add buttons, in one tap. Tick bills when they are paid, and tick money in when it lands. That is the whole daily habit: under a minute.

3 — The tools, in one line each

Envelopes give money a job inside your accounts. Categories are gentle colour limits, with rollover if you want it. Debt shows your debt-free date and lets you test extra payments. Goals move money out of spending, properly. Challenges save a little every week. Purchase pause makes impulse buys sleep on it. Subscription radar totals what quiet little payments cost a year. Statement check reads a bank file on this device and hunts forgotten subscriptions, duplicates and fees. Money back tracks refunds you are owed. Milestones keep your wins. And the Weekly money date walks you through a five-minute check-in, step by step. The forecast on the Today screen draws your balance day by day to payday and flags the hardest day before it arrives. A goal with a date gets a per-payday amount worked out for it. A yearly bill quietly sets its own money aside, month by month. And if money comes in from two places, both paydays count.

4 — Your money stays yours

No account, no bank login, no tracking, no server. Everything is stored on this device, in this file, and it works offline. Moving phones? Download a backup in Settings, then restore it on the new device. Keep a backup before clearing your browser, too.

5 — The honest limits

This is an organising tool, not financial advice, and not a medical product. It does not connect to your bank — the numbers are only as current as the last balance you confirmed, which is exactly why confirming takes one tap. It makes your money visible; the decisions stay yours.

Shared budget

One file, two people. Export this planner, send the file to your partner, and they import it on their phone or computer. No account, no cloud — the file itself is the sharing. When they send it back, import again to see their changes.

Honest note: this is not live sync. You pass the file back and forth — the newest import wins. That is the price of keeping everything private and offline.
